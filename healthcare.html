The shared challenge

Regulated AI asks the same three questions.

Financial services and healthcare differ in their rules, not in what a reviewer wants to see.

01 / Accountability

Financial servicesNamed senior individuals are accountable for AI-assisted decisions.

HealthcareDCB0129 (for manufacturers) and DCB0160 (for deploying organisations) set clinical risk management expectations for health IT.

02 / Evidence trails

Financial servicesA regulator can ask how one decision was made.

HealthcareClinical safety documentation, data protection impact assessments (DPIAs) and, for suppliers, DTAC all depend on clear records of what a system does and why.

03 / Human oversight

Both sectorsA person should be able to review, challenge and override an AI-assisted output, and you should be able to show that they did.

For public-sector transparency, the Algorithmic Transparency Recording Standard (ATRS) is the UK government’s standard for publishing information about algorithmic tools. It is mandatory for central government departments and certain arm’s-length bodies, and recommended more widely across the public sector.

Sources: DCB0129 and DCB0160 (NHS England); DTAC (NHS AI and Digital Regulations Service); DPIAs (Information Commissioner’s Office); ATRS (GOV.UK). This is a comparison of approaches, not legal advice. Standards change, so check the current version of each.

How we work

Controls built into systems,
not only written in policy.

A policy says what should happen. A control makes it happen, and leaves a record. It is the approach behind ComplyAI, our financial services system.

01 /

Verified evidence

Every claim is traced to a source you can inspect, and every quotation is checked against that source.

02 /

Human review

A person reviews each record before it is relied on.

03 /

Fail-closed checks

If a check cannot confirm something, the answer is no, not “probably”.

04 /

Audit trails

A record of who decided what, when and why.

IN DEVELOPMENT

Health AI Governance Evidence Index (working title)

We are building a way to gather public evidence of how health organisations govern AI, and to keep that evidence accurate. We have not published any findings.

  • Public sources only. robots.txt is respected.
  • Every quotation is checked against its source.
  • A person reviews every record before it is used.
  • Organisations, not people. The index does not record individuals.
  • Corrections are welcome.
